# Stakeholder Management

Product managers don't have authority over their stakeholders. They have influence. Stakeholder management is the practice of earning and maintaining that influence through clarity, consistency, and follow-through.

## The Stakeholder Map

For every major product decision, map:

| Stakeholder | What they care about | Their influence on the decision | Current stance |
|---|---|---|---|
| CEO / founder | Company strategy, growth | Veto power | Aligned / neutral / opposed |
| Engineering lead | Feasibility, tech debt, team health | Execution partner | — |
| Sales | Pipeline, deals, competitive gaps | Feature request source | — |
| Customer success | Retention, escalations | Voice of customer | — |
| Design | UX quality, consistency | Execution partner | — |
| Finance | Revenue, cost | Resource constraints | — |

Update this map before any roadmap review.

## Saying No Without Saying No

The goal is not to say no — it is to redirect to a better outcome.

**Frameworks for declining requests strategically:**

1. **Strategy redirect:** "That's a good idea. It optimizes for acquisition, and our current bet is on retention. Here's what would need to change in our strategy for that to move up."

2. **Later, not never:** "We've parked that in Next/Later. Here's what it would need to beat to move up — [RICE score or priority criteria]."

3. **Outcome reframe:** "What outcome are you trying to drive? Let me see if there's a better way to get there with what's already on the roadmap."

4. **Data ask:** "I'd want to validate that this is a widespread problem before prioritizing it. Can you share 3 examples from the last 30 days?"

## Managing Up

Leadership wants confidence, not updates.

**Confidence = evidence + plan + risk disclosure**

- Evidence: "We've validated this with 8 customer interviews and a prototype test."
- Plan: "Here's what we're building, in what sequence, and why."
- Risk: "The thing that could go wrong is X. Here's how we're managing it."

Leaders who trust the PM's process trust the roadmap. Leaders who don't trust the process micro-manage the features.

## Handling Executive Feature Requests

1. **Receive generously:** "That's interesting — tell me more about the problem you're seeing."
2. **Understand the job:** Is this a customer request? A competitive gap? An intuition?
3. **Score transparently:** "I'll run this through our prioritization framework and get back to you."
4. **Report back with data:** "I scored it at RICE X. That puts it at #8 in the backlog. Here's what it's behind and why."
5. **Offer the tradeoff:** "If you want this in Q2, here's what would need to move out."

Never just say no. Always show the trade-off.

## Roadmap Alignment Cadence

| Meeting | Audience | Frequency | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| Product review | Eng + Design | Weekly | Sprint alignment |
| Roadmap sync | Leadership | Monthly | Strategy alignment |
| Sales briefing | Sales + CS | Quarterly | Pipeline enablement |
| Customer advisory | Key customers | Quarterly | Feedback + direction |

## Common Rationalizations

| Rationalization | Reality |
|---|---|
| "Sales doesn't understand product priorities" | Sales sees the market every day. Understand their signal before dismissing their request. |
| "I just need to explain the roadmap better" | If stakeholders keep pushing back, the issue is trust, not communication. Earn trust with evidence. |
| "Executives should stay out of the roadmap" | Executives set strategy. Strategy informs roadmap. Their input is legitimate — manage it well. |
| "I can't say no to the CEO" | You can't say no arbitrarily. You can always present a trade-off and let them decide. |
