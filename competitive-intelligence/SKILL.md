# Competitive Intelligence

Competitive intelligence is not a feature comparison spreadsheet. It is an ongoing practice of understanding why customers choose you, why they choose competitors, and where the category is going.

## Win/Loss Analysis

The most valuable competitive intelligence comes from your own deals.

**Win interviews:** Call customers who chose you in a competitive evaluation.
- "What made you choose us over [competitor]?"
- "What almost made you go with [competitor]?"
- "What did [competitor] do better?"

**Loss interviews:** Call prospects who chose a competitor.
- "What made you go with [competitor] instead?"
- "What would have had to be true for you to choose us?"
- "What did we do better? (anything?)"

Do 5 wins and 5 losses per quarter. Patterns emerge fast.

## Battlecard Structure

One page per competitor, usable in a sales conversation:

**Section 1: When we see them**
- What deals do they show up in? (segment, size, use case)
- What are their sales triggers? (what do they lead with?)

**Section 2: Their strengths (honest)**
- What do they genuinely do better?
- What objections will you hear: "[competitor] has [capability] and you don't"?

**Section 3: Our advantages**
- Where do we win? Be specific and evidence-based.
- What proof points support each advantage? (case studies, G2 reviews, metrics)

**Section 4: Handle the comparison**
- If prospect says "[competitor] is cheaper": [response]
- If prospect says "[competitor] has [feature] and you don't": [response]
- The landmine question they will ask: [question + answer]

## Competitor Monitoring

Set up a system to catch competitor moves before they affect deals:

| Source | What it reveals | Cadence |
|---|---|---|
| G2 / Capterra reviews | Feature gaps, customer pain, churn signals | Weekly |
| LinkedIn job postings | Hiring priorities = product investment signals | Monthly |
| Product changelog / blog | What they're shipping | Weekly |
| Crunchbase / funding news | Strategic direction, runway | Monthly |
| Glassdoor | Internal culture, engineering capacity | Quarterly |
| Social listening | Customer sentiment, complaints | Weekly |

## Positioning Against Competitors

Never position by tearing down. Position by creating a different category or reframing the choice.

**Competitive reframe:** "If you need [what competitor is best at], they're a good choice. If you need [what you're best at], we're the right fit."

This works because:
1. It's honest — builds trust
2. It pre-qualifies the prospect
3. It anchors the comparison on your terms

## Common Rationalizations

| Rationalization | Reality |
|---|---|
| "We don't really have competitors" | Every buyer has an alternative. Name what they'd do if you didn't exist. |
| "Competitive analysis is a distraction" | Not knowing why you lose deals is a distraction. Win/loss analysis takes 2 hours/quarter. |
| "We shouldn't mention competitors in sales" | Prospects already know. Addressing the comparison builds credibility. |
| "Their G2 reviews don't apply to us" | G2 reviews for competitors reveal what customers in your category care about. |
