# Strategic Frameworks

Seven frameworks for strategic thinking — from business model validation to market analysis. Use the right tool for the question at hand.

## Framework Selection Guide

| Question | Framework |
|---|---|
| Is this business model viable? | Lean Canvas |
| What should we build next? | Opportunity Solution Tree |
| What could go wrong with this plan? | Pre-Mortem |
| How do we measure success? | North Star Metric |
| What external forces affect us? | PESTLE |
| Which markets should we enter? | Ansoff Matrix |
| How strong is our competitive position? | Porter's Five Forces |

## 1. Lean Canvas

**Use for:** Business model validation before building.

Adapted from Business Model Canvas. Replaces "Key Partners" with "Problem" and "Key Activities" with "Solution" to focus on risk, not operations.

**9 blocks to fill in order:**
1. **Problem** — Top 3 problems you're solving. Existing alternatives your customers use today.
2. **Customer Segments** — Who has this problem. Early adopters specifically.
3. **Unique Value Proposition** — Why you're different. "The only X that does Y without Z."
4. **Solution** — Top 3 features. Match one-to-one with problems.
5. **Channels** — How you reach customers. Free vs. paid, inbound vs. outbound.
6. **Revenue Streams** — How you make money. Pricing model and lifetime value.
7. **Cost Structure** — Your biggest costs. Fixed vs. variable.
8. **Key Metrics** —  2-3 numbers that tell you if the business is working.
9. **Unfair Advantage** — What cannot be easily copied or bought.

**Fill the canvas in 20 minutes. Then identify your riskiest assumption and test it first.**

## 2. Opportunity Solution Tree

**Use for:** Deciding what to build next.

```
Desired Outcome (the one metric that matters)
  └ Opportunity 1 (customer problem or unmet need)
      └ Solution A
      └ Solution B
  └ Opportunity 2
      └ Solution C
      └ Solution D
```

**Rules:**
- Start with ONE desired outcome. The tree collapses if you have two at the top.
- Opportunities come from customer research, not product ideas. Talk to customers before filling in opportunities.
- Solutions are hypotheses, not commitments. Map multiple solutions per opportunity.
- Pick the opportunity-solution pair with the best evidence and highest expected value.

## 3. Pre-Mortem

**Use for:** Identifying risks before a plan is executed.

**Exercise (60 minutes with the team):**
1. Imagine it is 12 months from now. The project has failed.
2. Each person writes 3-5 reasons it failed (individually, before group discussion).
3. Share all reasons. Group into themes.
4. For each theme: What is the probability? What is the impact? What mitigation exists?
5. Update the plan with mitigations for the top 3 risks.

**Why it works:** Prospective hindsight (imagining failure has already occurred) increases identification of correct reasons for failure by 30% (Klein, 2007).

## 4. North Star Metric

**Use for:** Aligning a team on a single measure of success.

**Criteria for a good North Star Metric:**
- It captures the core value delivered to customers (not just revenue)
- It predicts long-term revenue better than revenue itself
- It is understandable to everyone in the company
- It can be influenced by product decisions

**Examples by business type:**
| Business | North Star Metric |
|---|---|
| Social network | Daily active users |
| SaaS productivity | Weekly active teams |
| Marketplace | Successful transactions per month |
| Media / content | Total reading time per week |
| E-commerce | Repeat purchase rate |

**Input metrics (leading indicators that drive the NSM):**
For each North Star Metric, define 3-5 input metrics that teams can directly influence.

## 5. PESTLE Analysis

**Use for:** Understanding the macro environment before entering a market.

| Factor | Questions to answer |
|---|---|
| **Political** | How stable is the government? What regulations are pending? Trade policies? |
| **Economic** | GDP growth, inflation, unemployment, consumer spending trends. |
| **Social** | Demographics, cultural attitudes, education levels, lifestyle changes. |
| **Technological** | Emerging tech, R&D activity, automation, tech adoption rate. |
| **Legal** | Current laws affecting the industry (GDPR, PCI, employment law, IP). |
| **Environmental** | Climate regulations, sustainability expectations, supply chain risk. |

**Output:** Rank each factor by Impact (High/Med/Low) and Trend Direction (↑/→/↓). Focus strategy on High Impact × Moving factors.

## 6. Ansoff Matrix

**Use for:** Deciding which growth strategy to pursue.

| | Existing Products | New Products |
|---|---|---|
| **Existing Markets** | Market Penetration (lowest risk) | Product Development |
| **New Markets** | Market Development | Diversification (highest risk) |

**Risk order (lowest to highest):** Market Penetration < Product Development < Market Development < Diversification

**Guideline:** Exhaust market penetration before moving to adjacent quadrants. Most companies move too fast to new markets before saturating existing ones.

## 7. Porter's Five Forces

**Use for:** Assessing the structural attractiveness of an industry before entering.

| Force | High threat when... | Implication |
|---|---|---|
| **Threat of New Entrants** | Low capital requirements, no switching costs | Incumbents can't sustain margins |
| **Bargaining Power of Suppliers** | Few suppliers, high switching costs | Suppliers extract margin |
| **Bargaining Power of Buyers** | Many alternatives, low switching costs | Buyers extract margin |
| **Threat of Substitutes** | Alternative products meet the same need differently | Price ceiling on your product |
| **Competitive Rivalry** | Many similar-sized competitors, slow growth | Margin compression, price wars |

**Score each force 1–5 (1 = low threat, 5 = high threat). Sum < 15 = attractive industry. Sum > 20 = structurally difficult.**

## Common Rationalizations

| Rationalization | Reality |
|---|---|
| "We don't need frameworks, we know our business" | Frameworks force explicit assumptions. Unstated assumptions don't get challenged until they're wrong. |
| "The pre-mortem is too negative" | It's cheaper to find failure modes in a meeting than in production. |
| "We have multiple North Star Metrics" | Two north stars means no north star. Pick one. |
| "Porter's analysis says it's unattractive, but we'll differentiate" | Differentiation is a strategy, not an answer to structural forces. Know what you're competing against. |
