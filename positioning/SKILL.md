# Positioning

Positioning is not a tagline. It's the decision about which context makes your product obviously the right choice for the right buyer. Everything downstream depends on positioning being correct first.

## The Two-Layer Stack

**Layer 1 — Positioning (April Dunford):** How does this product fit into the market? What category does it win in? For whom is it obviously the best choice?

**Layer 2 — Brand philosophy (Seth Godin):** What does this brand believe? Who does it lead? What story does the customer tell themselves?

## Layer 1: April Dunford's Positioning Framework

### The 5 Components

**1. Competitive Alternatives** — What would the customer do if this product didn't exist? Not the feature-by-feature competitor list. The actual alternatives: Excel, hiring someone, doing nothing.

**2. Unique Attributes** — What does this product have that alternatives genuinely lack? Must be specific, verifiable, and differentiating.

**3. Value from Attributes** — For each unique attribute: what outcome does it enable? Attribute → capability → customer outcome.

**4. Best-Fit Customer Characteristics** — Who benefits most? Defined behaviorally: what situation are they in? What do they value? What alternatives do they use?

**5. Positioning Statement:**
```
For [best-fit customers] who [situation/need],
[product] is the [category frame]
that [unique value].
Unlike [competitive alternatives],
[product] [key differentiator].
```

### Positioning Stress-Tests

| Test | Pass condition |
|------|---------------|
| The swap test | Swap in a competitor's name — does it still work? (If yes, differentiation is too weak.) |
| The alternative test | Does the positioning clearly explain why this wins against each top alternative? |
| The best-fit test | Would 3 best-fit customers say "that's exactly what I need"? |
| The sales test | Would a salesperson say "I can work with this"? |

## Layer 2: Seth Godin's Brand Philosophy

### Smallest Viable Audience

Find the smallest audience that would truly miss the brand if it disappeared. Build for them obsessively.

- [ ] Can you describe 10 specific people who fit this profile?
- [ ] Do they have a shared worldview?
- [ ] Does the brand say something they've wanted someone to say?
- [ ] Would they tell others without being asked?

### The Brand Promise

The one thing a customer is guaranteed to get every time. About consistency, not aspiration.

### The Purple Cow

Remarkability is not optional. Before any campaign: is there anything here worth remarking on? If no: brand strategy work must happen before the campaign.

### Generosity Strategy

- What category knowledge does this brand have that competitors charge for?
- What can the brand teach that makes customers better at their jobs?
- What community resource can the brand create that would exist even if the product didn't?

## Key Messages Architecture

```
Primary claim: [single sentence capturing the positioned value]

Support points:
  - Claim: [support point] | Proof: [specific evidence]

Objections:
  - Objection: [top objection] | Response: [positioning-grounded response]

Segments:
  - Segment: [ICP] | Tailored claim: [how primary claim is reframed for this segment]
```

## Common Rationalizations

| Rationalization | Reality |
|---|---|
| "Our positioning is our tagline" | A tagline is the output of positioning, not the positioning itself. |
| "We position differently to different segments" | You have one positioning. You may have segment-specific messaging derived from it. |
| "The competitive alternatives are just the named competitors" | What the customer would do if the product didn't exist often isn't the obvious competitor. |
| "Positioning is marketing's job" | Positioning is a company-wide decision. Sales, product, and marketing must all live in it. |
