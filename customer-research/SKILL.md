# Customer Research

Expert knowledge for planning, conducting, and synthesizing customer research that informs positioning, messaging, and product direction.

## Why Customer Research is Marketing's Most Underused Lever

The best marketing copy is written in the customer's own words. Research reveals:
- The exact language customers use to describe their problem
- The trigger event that made them look for a solution
- The fears they had before buying
- Why they chose you over alternatives (your real differentiator)

## Research Methods by Goal

| Goal | Best Method |
|---|---|
| Understand why customers buy | Jobs-to-be-Done interviews |
| Improve onboarding and activation | User interviews + session recordings |
| Find product-market fit signals | Survey (NPS + open-ended) |
| Understand why deals are lost | Win/loss interviews |
| Define ICP characteristics | Customer data analysis + interviews |
| Validate messaging | 5-second test, preference tests |

## Jobs-to-be-Done (JTBD) Framework

Customers don't buy products — they hire them to do a job.

**Three layers:**
1. **Functional job:** The practical task
2. **Social job:** How they want to be perceived
3. **Emotional job:** How they want to feel

### JTBD Interview Script

**The timeline interview (work backwards from purchase):**
1. "Take me back to when you first realized you needed something different. What was happening?"
2. "What triggered that realization?"
3. "What did you do first when you decided to look for solutions?"
4. "What other options did you consider? Why those?"
5. "What made you choose [product] over those alternatives?"
6. "What were you worried about before you bought?"
7. "What would have made you not buy?"
8. "How has your life/work changed since using [product]?"

**The magic question:** "If [product] disappeared tomorrow and you couldn't use it, what would you do?"

## User Interview Best Practices

- 5-8 interviews per segment (more don't add proportional value after 5)
- Interview recent buyers (within 90 days) — memory is fresh
- Ask about the past, not the future
- Silence is a tool — pause and let them fill it
- Never ask leading questions
- Never ask hypothetical questions

## Transcript Synthesis

1. Highlight quotes that describe pain, trigger events, fears, benefits, success metrics
2. Extract to observations: verbatim quote + customer type + category
3. Group into themes
4. Count frequency — themes in 3+ of 5 interviews are insights
5. Write insight statements: "[N] of [total] customers said [theme]"

## Win/Loss Analysis

**Questions:**
1. "Can you walk me through how you evaluated your options?"
2. "What were the top 3 things you considered?"
3. "What almost made you choose a different vendor?"
4. "What were you worried about with us?"
5. [For losses] "What would have changed your decision?"

## ICP Definition from Research

- **Firmographic:** Company size, industry, geography, funding stage
- **Technographic:** Tech stack, tools they currently use
- **Psychographic:** Attitudes, priorities, decision-making style
- **Behavioral:** How they evaluate software, buying process
- **Situational:** Trigger events that make them ready to buy

**ICP validation:** Plot best customers (highest LTV, lowest churn, most referrals) on each dimension. Where they cluster = confirmed ICP.

## Common Rationalizations

| Rationalization | Reality |
|---|---|
| "We already know our customers — we built the product" | Founders' mental model diverges from reality within months of launch. |
| "5 interviews isn't enough data" | 5-8 interviews reveal 80% of the patterns. |
| "Surveys are faster and cheaper" | Surveys quantify; they don't explain. You need interviews to know what to survey. |
| "We'll do research after we fix the obvious product issues" | Research tells you what the real issues are. |
