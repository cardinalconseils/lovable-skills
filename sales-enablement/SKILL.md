# Sales Enablement

Expert knowledge for creating sales materials that move deals forward: pitch decks, one-pagers, battle cards, objection handling frameworks, and demo scripts.

## Pitch Deck Structure (9 Slides)

The best pitch decks tell a story in sequence. Every slide earns the next one.

| Slide | Title | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Problem | Make the audience feel the pain. Data + relatable scenario. |
| 2 | Why Now | Why is this the moment? Market shift, tech change, regulation. |
| 3 | Solution | What you do. One clear sentence + product visual. |
| 4 | How It Works | 3-step process. Not a feature list — a workflow. |
| 5 | Traction / Proof | Best metric you have: ARR, customers, case study result. |
| 6 | Market Size | TAM / SAM / SOM. Be specific — vague TAMs kill credibility. |
| 7 | Business Model | How you make money. Pricing, ACV, unit economics if known. |
| 8 | Team | Why this team for this problem. Domain expertise + execution proof. |
| 9 | Ask | What you want: dollar amount, use of funds, timeline. Be specific. |

**Sales pitch variant (not investor):** Remove Market Size and Ask. Add ROI evidence and a case study. Compress to 6 slides.

## One-Pager Layout

A one-pager is used to leave behind after a meeting or attach to an email. It answers: what do you do, who is it for, why does it work, what does it cost, and what should I do next.

**Layout (top to bottom):**
1. **Headline:** Outcome-first. "Cut reporting time by 80%" not "Introducing [Product]"
2. **Problem (2 sentences):** The pain the buyer knows they have.
3. **Solution (2 sentences):** What you do and how it solves the problem.
4. **How It Works (3 bullets):** Step-by-step, buyer's perspective.
5. **Social Proof:** One specific result from a named or similar customer.
6. **Pricing:** Starting price or pricing structure. Don't hide it.
7. **CTA:** One clear next step with contact information.

## Battle Card

A battle card is an internal reference tool for sales reps. It is NOT shown to the customer.

**Structure:**
- **Competitor:** [Name + one-line description]
- **When we win:** The 2-3 scenarios where we consistently beat them
- **When they win:** The 2-3 scenarios where they genuinely beat us (be honest)
- **Their pitch:** How they position against us (use their words if possible)
- **Our counter:** Specific reframe, not just "but we're better"
- **Proof point:** A case study or stat that backs our counter
- **Objection handling:** Top 3 objections a rep will hear and the response

**Update cadence:** Battle cards expire in 90 days. Competitor products change. Stale battle cards lose deals.

## Objection Handling Framework

Four steps, always in order: Acknowledge → Clarify → Address → Confirm.

**Template:**
```
Acknowledge: "I hear you — [restate objection in their words]."
Clarify:     "Can I ask — is that because [assumption A] or [assumption B]?"
Address:     "For customers who've had that concern, what's worked is [specific evidence]."
Confirm:     "Does that address what you were worried about, or is there more to it?"
```

**Common objections by category:**

| Objection | Real Concern | Address With |
|---|---|---|
| "It's too expensive" | ROI not clear | ROI calculation tied to their numbers |
| "We'll build it ourselves" | Underestimate complexity | Time-to-value comparison, opportunity cost |
| "We're happy with [competitor]" | Risk of switching | Migration support + risk reversal offer |
| "We need to think about it" | Missing champion internally | Identify who else needs to be in the room |
| "Come back next quarter" | Budget cycle timing | Connect to a business event happening sooner |

## Demo Script Framework

**Discovery first.** A demo without discovery is a feature presentation. Spend the first 10 minutes learning before showing anything.

**Discovery questions:**
1. "What's your current workflow for [relevant process]?"
2. "What's the biggest pain point in that workflow today?"
3. "What does success look like for you in 6 months?"

**Demo structure:**
1. **Hook (2 min):** Show the single most impressive thing first. Don't build to it.
2. **Their problem (3 min):** Narrate their pain using their words from discovery.
3. **Your solution (10 min):** Walk through their specific scenario — not a general feature tour.
4. **Proof (2 min):** Show one result from a similar customer.
5. **Next step (3 min):** Ask for a specific commitment. Never end with "let me know if you have questions."

## ROI Calculator Structure

The goal: make it easy for the champion to build the internal business case.

**Inputs (buyer fills in):**
- Number of [relevant role] employees
- Average [relevant activity] hours per week
- Average fully-loaded hourly cost
- Current [metric they care about] rate

**Outputs (auto-calculated):**
- Hours saved per week / year
- Dollar value of time saved
- Estimated improvement in [metric]
- Payback period at your price

**Rule:** Use conservative assumptions. An ROI calculator that inflates benefits creates buyer skepticism. Let them adjust inputs and see the math.

## Common Rationalizations

| Rationalization | Reality |
|---|---|
| "Buyers want features, not outcomes" | Buyers want to solve a problem. Features are proof, outcomes are the message. |
| "Our product is too complex for a one-pager" | A one-pager with footnotes is always better than no one-pager. Buyers won't read a 10-page brochure. |
| "We'll respond to objections as they come" | Ad-hoc objection responses lose deals. Prepare the top 5 in advance. |
| "Demo first, discovery later" | You can't demo their problem if you don't know what it is. Discovery is non-negotiable. |
