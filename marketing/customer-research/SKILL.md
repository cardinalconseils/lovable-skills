# Customer Research

Expert knowledge for planning, conducting, and synthesizing customer research that informs positioning, messaging, and product direction.

## Why Customer Research is Marketing's Most Underused Lever

The best marketing copy is written in the customer's own words. Research reveals:
- The exact language customers use to describe their problem (use verbatim in headlines)
- The trigger event that made them look for a solution (use in email subjects)
- The fears they had before buying (address in the sales process)
- Why they chose you over alternatives (your real differentiator)

## Research Methods by Goal

| Goal | Best Method |
|---|---|
| Understand why customers buy | Jobs-to-be-Done interviews |
| Improve onboarding and activation | User interviews + session recordings |
| Find product-market fit signals | Survey (NPS + open-ended) |
| Understand why deals are lost | Win/loss interviews |
| Validate messaging | 5-second test, preference tests |

## Jobs-to-be-Done (JTBD) Framework

Customers don't buy products — they hire them to do a job.

**Three layers of the job:**
1. **Functional:** The practical task ("generate monthly reports faster")
2. **Social:** How they want to be perceived ("look organized to my manager")
3. **Emotional:** How they want to feel ("feel in control of our data")

### JTBD Interview Script

**Session setup:** 45-60 minutes, recorded (with permission).

**Opening:** "I want to understand the process you went through when you decided to look for a solution like [product]. I'm not going to pitch anything."

**Timeline interview (work backwards from the purchase):**
1. "Take me back to when you first realized you needed something different. What was happening?"
2. "What triggered that realization? Was there a specific event?"
3. "What did you do first when you decided to look for solutions?"
4. "What other options did you consider? Why did you look at those?"
5. "What made you choose [product] over those alternatives?"
6. "What were you worried about before you bought?"
7. "What would have made you not buy?"
8. "How has your life/work changed since using [product]?"

**The magic question (ask in every interview):**
"If [product] disappeared tomorrow and you couldn't use it, what would you do?"

## Interview Best Practices

- Ask about the past, not the future ("What did you do?" not "What would you do?")
- Silence is a tool — pause after answers and let them fill it
- Never ask leading questions
- Never pitch your product during the interview

## Transcript Synthesis

1. **Highlight quotes** — pain in customer's own words, trigger events, fears, benefits
2. **Extract to observations** — verbatim quote + customer type + category
3. **Group into themes** — cluster similar observations
4. **Count frequency** — themes in 3+ of 5 interviews are insights
5. **Write insight statements** — "[N] of [total] customers said [theme]. Example: [quote 1], [quote 2]"

## Win/Loss Analysis

**Win/loss interview questions:**
1. "Can you walk me through how you evaluated your options?"
2. "What were the top 3 things you considered?"
3. "What almost made you choose a different vendor?"
4. "What were you worried about with us?"
5. [For losses] "What would have changed your decision?"

## The One Question That Predicts PMF

"On a scale of 0-10, how disappointed would you be if you could no longer use [product]?"
- > 40% say "very disappointed" → product-market fit signal
- < 40% → more work needed

## Common Rationalizations

| Rationalization | Reality |
|---|---|
| "We already know our customers" | Founders' mental model diverges from reality within months of launch. Research is a reset. |
| "5 interviews isn't enough data" | 5-8 interviews reveal 80% of patterns. 50 interviews don't add proportional insight. |
| "Surveys are faster and cheaper than interviews" | Surveys quantify; they don't explain. You need interviews to know what to survey. |
