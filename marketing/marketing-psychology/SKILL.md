# Marketing Psychology & Behavioral Science

Expert knowledge for applying psychological principles and behavioral economics to marketing copy, design, and campaigns.

## Cialdini's 7 Principles of Influence

### 1. Reciprocity
People feel obligated to return favors. Give first.
**Applications:** Free resources (lead magnets, templates); free trials that deliver real value before asking for a credit card.

### 2. Commitment and Consistency
People act in ways consistent with prior commitments. Get small yeses first.
**Applications:** Quiz funnels; free trial → paid trial → annual plan; interactive calculators where users input their data.

### 3. Social Proof
When uncertain, people look to others' behavior.

| Type | Power | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Expert endorsement | High | "Recommended by [authority]" |
| Customer testimonials | High | Specific outcome + customer name |
| User counts | Medium | "Join 50,000+ teams" |
| Ratings and reviews | Medium | "4.8/5 from 1,200 reviews" |

**Social proof matching:** Match proof to ICP identity — a mid-market prospect is more persuaded by a similar company than a Fortune 500.

### 4. Authority
People defer to credible experts.
**Applications:** Author credentials; media mentions; data-backed claims; thought leadership content.

### 5. Liking
People say yes to people they like.
**Applications:** Authentic brand voice; shared values; founder-led content; customer spotlights.

### 6. Scarcity
Things that are limited feel more valuable.
**Legitimate scarcity:** Limited beta access; limited-time pricing with a real deadline.
**Warning:** Fake scarcity damages trust when discovered.

### 7. Unity
People are influenced by those they see as part of their group.
**Applications:** "For [specific identity] by [specific identity]" positioning; ICP-specific messaging.

## Kahneman: System 1 and System 2

**System 1:** Fast, automatic, emotional. Pattern recognition. Gut feeling.
**System 2:** Slow, deliberate, rational. Analysis. Critical thinking.

Most buying decisions are made by System 1 and justified by System 2.

**Practical:** Design your hero section for System 1. Support with System 2 content below the fold.

## Key Cognitive Biases

### Loss Aversion
Losses feel 2× more painful than equivalent gains feel good.
- "Don't lose your data" > "Save your data"
- Trial expiry warnings ("Your free access ends in 3 days")
- Risk reversal: "Money-back guarantee"

### Anchoring
The first number encountered heavily influences subsequent judgments.
- Show the highest tier first (enterprise plan as the anchor)
- Show annual price before monthly
- Show crossed-out original price next to sale price

### The Framing Effect

| Equivalent frames | Effect |
|---|---|
| "90% success rate" vs "10% failure rate" | Same fact, different feel |
| "Save 20 hours/month" vs "20 hours saved per month" | Action-oriented vs passive |
| "Only $4/day" vs "$120/month" | Same price, different salience |

### The Decoy Effect
Adding a third option makes one of the other two look better.

| Plan | Price | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Basic | $29/mo | Anchor |
| **Pro (target)** | **$79/mo** | Looks like the smart choice |
| Enterprise | $89/mo | Makes Pro look like a steal |

### The Zeigarnik Effect
People feel compelled to complete uncompleted tasks.
- Onboarding progress bars ("Complete your profile — 60% done")
- "You're almost ready" prompts

## The Behavioral Design Stack

1. Default to the desired behavior (pre-select recommended plan)
2. Reduce friction for desired actions
3. Add friction to undesired actions (confirm before cancel)
4. Use social norms ("Most customers choose...")
5. Make the desired path the obvious path

## Ethical Use

**Use psychology to:** Help people make decisions that are genuinely good for them; remove friction; surface real value.

**Never use psychology to:** Create false urgency or scarcity; use dark patterns; manipulate into purchases they'll regret.

## Common Rationalizations

| Rationalization | Reality |
|---|---|
| "Our customers are rational" | All customers are human. System 1 precedes System 2 in every buying decision. |
| "Adding urgency always helps" | Fake urgency destroys trust when discovered. |
| "Social proof doesn't matter in B2B" | B2B buyers have career risk. Social proof reduces perceived risk. |
