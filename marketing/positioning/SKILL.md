# Positioning

Positioning is the decision about which context makes your product obviously the right choice for the right buyer. Everything downstream — messaging, copy, campaigns, community — depends on positioning being correct first.

## The Two-Layer Stack

**Layer 1 — Positioning (April Dunford):** How does this product fit into the market? What category does it win in?

**Layer 2 — Brand philosophy (Seth Godin):** What does this brand believe? Who does it lead? What story does the customer tell themselves?

## Layer 1: April Dunford's Positioning Framework

### The 5 Components of Positioning

**1. Competitive Alternatives**
What would the customer do if this product didn't exist? Not the feature-by-feature competitor list — the actual alternatives: Excel, hiring someone, doing nothing.

Test: if a customer churned tomorrow, what would they use instead?

**2. Unique Attributes**
What does this product have that alternatives genuinely lack?
- Specific (not "better support" — "24/7 live chat with <2 min response time")
- Verifiable
- Differentiating

**3. Value from Attributes**
For each unique attribute: what outcome does it enable? Chain: attribute → capability → customer outcome.

**4. Best-Fit Customer Characteristics**
Who benefits most? Defined behaviorally: what situation are they in? What do they value? What alternatives do they currently use?

**5. Positioning Statement**
```
For [best-fit customers] who [situation/need],
[product] is the [category frame]
that [unique value].
Unlike [competitive alternatives],
[product] [key differentiator].
```

### Positioning Stress-Tests

| Test | Pass condition |
|------|---------------|
| The swap test | Swap in a competitor's name — does the statement still work? (If yes, differentiation is too weak.) |
| The alternative test | Does positioning clearly explain why this wins against each alternative? |
| The best-fit test | Would 3 best-fit customers say "that's exactly what I need"? |
| The sales test | Does a salesperson say "I can work with this"? |

## Layer 2: Seth Godin's Brand Philosophy

### Smallest Viable Audience

Find the smallest audience that would truly miss the brand if it disappeared. Build for them obsessively.

Checklist:
- [ ] Can you describe 10 specific people who fit this profile?
- [ ] Do they share a worldview — things they believe that others don't say out loud?
- [ ] Does the brand say something they've wanted someone to say?
- [ ] Would they tell others without being asked?

### The Brand Promise

The one thing a customer is guaranteed to get every time they interact with this brand. About consistency, not aspiration.

Test: Does every touchpoint — product, support, content, sales process — deliver this promise?

### The Purple Cow

Remarkability is not optional. Advertising a mediocre product makes it fail faster.

The question before any campaign: is there anything here worth remarking on?

### Generosity Strategy

- What category knowledge does this brand have that competitors charge for?
- What can the brand teach that makes customers better at their jobs (not just better at using the product)?

## Community Development Framework

**Phase 1 — Define the tribe:** Who belongs? The people who share the belief that makes this brand exist.

**Phase 2 — Create the minimum viable community:** One place where the tribe gathers. Not five channels — one.

**Phase 3 — Lead with agenda, not promotion:** Teacher and curator role, not promoter.

**Phase 4 — Give members status:** Spotlight customers, create contribution pathways.

## Key Messages Architecture

```
Primary claim: [single sentence that captures the positioned value]

Support points:
  - Claim: [support point 1] | Proof: [specific evidence]
  - Claim: [support point 2] | Proof: [specific evidence]

Objections:
  - Objection: [top customer objection] | Response: [positioning-grounded response]
```

## Common Rationalizations

| Rationalization | Reality |
|---|---|
| "Our positioning is our tagline" | A tagline is the output of positioning, not the positioning itself. |
| "We position differently to different segments" | You have one positioning. You may have segment-specific messaging derived from it. |
| "The competitive alternatives are just the named competitors" | What the customer would do if the product didn't exist often isn't the obvious competitor. |
| "Positioning is marketing's job" | Positioning is a company-wide decision. Sales, product, and marketing must all live in it. |
