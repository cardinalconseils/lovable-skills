# Market Mapping

Maps who's in the space, where profit concentrates, and who the real customers are — before committing to a position.

## Market Mapping Philosophy

**Profit ≠ revenue.** The largest player by revenue is often not extracting the most margin. Map where profit sits.

**Incumbents ≠ real competition.** Your real competitors are whoever your best customers would choose if you didn't exist.

**Segment by behavior, not demographics.** "SMB marketing teams" is a demographic. "Teams who currently manage 3+ agencies and spend 20h/week on reporting" is a behavioral segment.

## Player Categories

**Tier 1 Incumbents** — define the rules, pricing norms, and default buyer expectation. Dominant in the core, losing at the edges.

**Tier 2 Challengers** — define the disruption vector. Where incumbents are slow, they're fast. Watch what they're building.

**Adjacent Players** — different business model, same customer pain. The most underestimated threat category.

**Emerging Threats** — pre-product or very early. Follow the venture money and conference talk titles.

For each category: name 2-3 representative players, their business model in one line, and what they're optimizing for.

## Profit Pool Analysis

1. Identify every role in the value chain (supplier → builder → distributor → customer success → end user)
2. Estimate gross margin at each stage (use public comps, 10-K filings, or proxy signals)
3. Find the stage where margin concentrates — that's where bargaining power lives
4. Distinguish: who creates value vs who extracts value

**Proxy signals when hard data is unavailable:**
- Fundraising multiples
- Pricing power (can they raise prices without losing customers?)
- Customer acquisition cost relative to LTV
- Churn rate (low churn → value delivered)

## Customer Segmentation

Segment by three axes:
- **Pain intensity** — how much does this hurt, measured in money or time lost per month?
- **Willingness to pay** — what budget exists, and who controls it?
- **Reachability** — how do you get to them, and how expensive is that motion?

The segment worth targeting: highest pain × WTP × reachability — not the biggest TAM slice.

## White Space Detection

White space = a gap where:
- Pain is high (actively complaining, paying for workarounds)
- Solutions are weak (incumbent tools are 10+ years old, complex, expensive)
- No dominant player has locked in the segment

White space must be specific enough to build a product thesis against it.

## Research Process

1. List every player you know in the space (10-20 companies). Don't filter yet.
2. Research each: business model, primary customer, pricing, what best customers love, what churned customers complain about.
3. Group into tiers (Incumbent / Challenger / Adjacent / Emerging).
4. For each tier, name the #1 company and describe what they optimize for.
5. Apply the profit pool analysis.
6. Apply the 3-axis segmentation.
7. Name the white space with enough specificity to build against it.

**Sources:** Company websites, G2/Capterra reviews (read the negative reviews), LinkedIn job postings, Crunchbase, S-1 filings.

## Common Rationalizations

| Rationalization | Reality |
|---|---|
| "Our market is too new to map" | New markets are undefended segments of old markets — find the adjacent segment. |
| "All segments look attractive" | Apply the pain × WTP × reachability filter. |
| "We compete with everyone" | Name the 2-3 players your best customers would choose if you didn't exist. |
| "Profit pool analysis requires data we don't have" | Use proxy signals: fundraising multiples, pricing power, CAC vs LTV. |

## Quality Bar

- [ ] Player categories describe behavior and business model, not just company names
- [ ] Profit pool is distinct from revenue map — margin concentration identified
- [ ] Target segment defined by pain + WTP + reachability, not demographics alone
- [ ] White space is specific enough to build a product thesis against it
