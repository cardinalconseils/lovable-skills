# Competitor Alternative & Comparison Pages

Expert knowledge for creating high-converting competitor alternative pages that rank for "alternative to X" queries and win deals at the bottom of the funnel.

## Why These Pages Matter

"Alternative to [competitor]" queries are among the highest-intent searches on the internet. Someone searching for alternatives has already decided they want to buy.

These pages serve two purposes:
1. **SEO:** Capture bottom-of-funnel search traffic
2. **Sales enablement:** Give prospects an honest, structured comparison

## Page Types

**Alternative page:** "Best [Category] alternatives to [Competitor]" — broader keyword coverage, more credible when listing multiple options.

**Vs page:** "[Your Product] vs [Competitor]" — head-to-head format, higher conversion when reader has narrowed to two options.

**Category landing page:** "Best [category software] in [year]" — earns backlinks, targets "best [category]" keywords.

## Page Structure: Alternative Page

### Hero Section
- H1: "Best [Competitor] Alternatives in [Year]"
- Do not start with a negative attack. Start with objective framing.

### Why People Look for Alternatives (100-150 words)
Describe legitimate reasons. Be fair — honesty about competitor strengths makes your critique more credible.

### The Alternatives
For each alternative (including yourself):
- **Best for:** [specific use case]
- **Pricing:** [general pricing]
- **Key strength:** [what they genuinely do well]
- **Key limitation:** [honest limitation]

List 4-6 alternatives including yourself. If you list only yourself, it's an ad.

### Comparison Table

| Feature | [Your Product] | [Competitor A] | [Competitor B] |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Feature 1] | ✓ | ✗ | ✓ |

## Page Structure: Vs Page

### TL;DR Summary Box
"[Your Product] is better for: [specific use case]"
"[Competitor] is better for: [specific use case]"

Pretending you're better at everything is a credibility killer.

### Switching Section
- How long does migration take?
- Do you offer migration assistance?
- What's the risk if it doesn't work out?

## Fair Framing Principles

1. Never make false claims about the competitor.
2. Acknowledge legitimate strengths.
3. Use current pricing and features.
4. Date the page ("Last updated: [Month Year]").

## Common Rationalizations

| Rationalization | Reality |
|---|---|
| "We shouldn't mention competitors" | Buyers are already searching for these comparisons. If you're not the answer, a competitor is. |
| "We'll just say we're better in every way" | Savvy buyers distrust one-sided comparisons. Honest assessments convert better. |
| "Our product is too complex to compare in a table" | A table with footnotes is always better than no comparison. |

## Verification

- [ ] Primary keyword researched and confirmed with search volume data
- [ ] Competitor information current (pricing and features verified within 30 days)
- [ ] Page includes fair acknowledgment of competitor strengths
- [ ] Comparison table covers features where your product genuinely wins
- [ ] FAQ section with schema markup implemented
- [ ] Switching/migration section addresses cost and risk
- [ ] CTA placed above fold and after each major section
