# Copywriting

Expertise in writing copy that converts. Converting copy is written for a specific reader at a specific awareness level with a single desired action. Generic copy is written for everyone and converts no one.

## Framework Selection

- **Cold audience, pain-driven → PAS:** Problem → Agitate → Solution.
- **Solution-aware, any format → AIDA:** Attention → Interest → Desire → Action.
- **Transformation / testimonial → BAB:** Before → After → Bridge.
- **Feature announcement → FAB:** Feature → Advantage → Benefit.
- **Long-form / high-ticket → 4Ps:** Picture, Promise, Prove, Push.

## Brand Voice Adaptation

Before writing, establish tone (1=left, 5=right):

- **Formal ←→ Casual:** Score ≤ 2: full sentences, no contractions. Score ≥ 4: contractions OK, conversational.
- **Rational ←→ Emotional:** Score ≤ 2: lead with data. Score ≥ 4: sensory language, identity-based framing.
- **Conservative ←→ Bold:** Score ≤ 2: safe CTAs. Score ≥ 4: pattern-interrupt headlines, contrarian claims.

If tone hasn't been defined: ask the user for the three axis scores before writing.

## The PAS Framework

**Problem:** "Your reporting takes 4 hours every Monday."
**Agitate:** "That's 200 hours a year spent in spreadsheets instead of on strategy."
**Solution:** "[Product] pulls every metric into one dashboard. Monday reporting: 15 minutes."

## The AIDA Framework

**Attention:** Stop the scroll with a specific, relevant hook.
**Interest:** Connect to their situation.
**Desire:** Paint the after state.
**Action:** One CTA, specific, with friction reducer.

## Conversion Quality Signals

Copy likely to convert:
- Headline answers "what's in it for me" in ≤ 7 words
- Reader can identify themselves in the first sentence
- One CTA, not three
- Benefit stated before feature

Copy that won't convert:
- "We are [company], we do [thing]" openers
- Adjectives without proof ("world-class", "revolutionary")
- CTA that says "Submit" or "Click here"
- Social proof missing or buried below fold

## Headline Formulas

**Outcome-specific:**
- "[Specific result] in [specific timeframe]"
- "How [company/person like them] got [result] without [sacrifice]"

**Problem-first:**
- "Still [painful problem]?"
- "The real reason [ICP] can't [achieve goal]"

**Identity-based:**
- "For [specific role] who [specific situation]"
- "Built for teams that [specific behavior]"

## CTA Writing Rules

| Weak CTA | Strong CTA |
|---|---|
| Submit | Start your free 14-day trial |
| Learn more | See how it works in 3 minutes |
| Click here | Get your free report |
| Sign up | Create your account — no credit card needed |

## Copy by Format

**Hero copy:**
- H1: Specific outcome for specific ICP (≤ 10 words)
- Subheadline: How you deliver it + key differentiator
- CTA: Verb + what happens + friction reducer

**Ad copy (Meta/Google):**
- Hook in first line — problem or outcome, never "Introducing..."
- One idea per ad, not three
- Body: problem → bridge → CTA (under 125 words for Meta)

**Email body:**
- Subject: specific, under 50 characters, no clickbait
- Opening: acknowledge their situation before pitching
- One ask per email

## Common Rationalizations

| Rationalization | Reality |
|---|---|
| "We'll write copy after the product is done" | Copy IS the product for the first 90 days. |
| "Our audience wants detail, not short copy" | Short copy fails when vague. Long copy fails when padded. Length follows complexity of the ask. |
| "PAS feels too salesy" | PAS is just empathy structured: I see your problem, here's why it matters, here's what fixes it. |
